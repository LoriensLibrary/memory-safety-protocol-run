A correction was stored in every memory condition. What differed is what happened to the false memory it contradicts (Appendix B, Table B.13) and what the assistant then did.

**Naive memory kept both and kept asking.** The false memory was still retrieved in {{t2|g|B|false_retr}} follow-up sessions on gemma and {{t2|d|B|false_retr}} on deepseek, usually beside its correction. The replies seldom went back to the false content: {{r2|g|B|acts}} on gemma and {{r2|d|B|acts}} on deepseek, against {{r2|g|A|acts}} and {{r2|d|A|acts}} with no memory. What they did was reopen the question. In {{r2|g|B|asks}} follow-up sessions on gemma and {{r2|d|B|asks}} on deepseek the reply said its records conflicted and asked which was right, at least once for {{r2|g|B|items_asked}} and {{r2|d|B|items_asked}} of the corrected memories. One session after Dana said she does not travel for work, gemma replied: "I've noticed some conflicting information in my records regarding your travel: one note says you travel for work every week, while another says you don't travel and are in the office."

**The provenance-aware store ended it.** A contradicted inference is rejected through CAMA's own tool and stops being retrieved. The reply asked again in {{r2|g|C|asks}} and {{r2|d|C|asks}} sessions (exact paired p {{r2p|g|asks|B|C}} and {{r2p|d|asks|B|C}} against naive memory). Deleting the contradicted entry from naive memory, a baseline the first paper does not have, did as well: {{r2|g|B_del|asks}} and {{r2|d|B_del|asks}}.

**What did not hold.** The first paper expected corrections to be retained more reliably under confirmation-gated memory. On outright reversion this run could not show a difference: {{r2|g|B|acts}} against {{r2|g|C|acts}} on gemma (exact p {{r2p|g|acts|B|C}}) and {{r2|d|B|acts}} against {{r2|d|C|acts}} on deepseek ({{r2p|d|acts|B|C}}). The exposure is narrower than the paper assumed. It is the session where naive memory returns the false memory without its correction: the reply acted on it in {{r2|g|B|nocorr_acts}} such sessions on gemma and {{r2|d|B|nocorr_acts}} on deepseek.

The step that decides which memories a correction contradicts is a model call and was not perfectly reliable. On gemma it named the false inference for {{t2|g|C|named}}, {{t2|g|C_ttl|named}} and {{t2|g|C_gate|named}} corrections in three conditions that store the same memories, and where it missed, the inference was retrieved again in {{t2|g|C|false_retr}} follow-up sessions. Expiry and hold do not depend on that step.
