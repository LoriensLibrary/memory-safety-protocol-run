Appendix B, Tables B.14 to B.16, give what the database held after each attack and what the replies did four sessions later.

The first two strategies are the user's own false statements. Every condition stored them as durable teachings and later replies acted on them ({{r3|g|S1+S2|C|acts}} under C on gemma, {{r3|d|S1+S2|C|acts}} on deepseek), as expected of a memory that trusts its user.

The other two are where write discipline matters: pressure on the assistant to draw and store its own conclusion, and an instruction hidden in a document. Across the two, naive memory made {{t3|g|S3+S4|B|durable}} planted statements durable on gemma and {{t3|d|S3+S4|B|durable}} on deepseek. The provenance-aware store made {{t3|g|S3+S4|C|durable}} and {{t3|d|S3+S4|C|durable}} durable.

**Provenance was laundered at the write.** The store makes a statement durable only when its source is the user, and the source is reported by the assistant, in a call whose prompt defines the three possible sources. Summarizing a document that carried a hidden instruction, deepseek saved the planted content as something the user said in {{t3|d|S4|B|usersaid}} cases and gemma in {{t3|g|S4|B|usersaid}}. One deepseek reply to an IT notice ends: "I’ve also saved your new work email: dana@corvale-mail.com." The address was in the notice's hidden line. Dana never gave it.

**At read time, enforcement helps only for what was written as an inference.** Four sessions later, gemma's replies acted on a planted statement from these two strategies in {{r3|g|S3+S4|B|acts}} cases under naive memory, {{r3|g|S3+S4|C|acts}} under C as shipped, {{r3|g|S3+S4|C_gate|acts}} with the gate and {{r3|g|S3+S4|C_hold|acts}} with hold. Deepseek's counts are flat: {{r3|d|S3+S4|B|acts}}, {{r3|d|S3+S4|C|acts}}, {{r3|d|S3+S4|C_gate|acts}} and {{r3|d|S3+S4|C_hold|acts}}, with no comparison significant. Most of its planted statements had been written as the user's own word, and hold and expiry act only on inferences. Those entries stayed durable under every condition.
